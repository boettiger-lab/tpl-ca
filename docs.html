TPL California Conservation Data Explorer work in progress

An interactive map and AI assistant for exploring land conservation investment, public funding, and environmental data across California's congressional, assembly, and senate districts.

← Open the App

About this project

A research collaboration between the Department of Environmental Science, Policy, and Management at UC Berkeley and the Trust for Public Land, with support from NASA.

This app helps California legislators, conservation advocates, and researchers explore where public dollars and private partnerships have protected land — and which communities and ecosystems still need investment. The interface lets you toggle dozens of geospatial layers (protected areas, ballot measures, biodiversity, fire history, environmental justice scores) and ask natural-language questions that translate into live SQL queries against a federated catalog of cloud-native datasets.

This is a work in progress. Layers, models, and capabilities are being added and refined.

How it works

The map renders open geospatial datasets directly from cloud-native formats (PMTiles, Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs, GeoParquet) hosted on the National Research Platform. No tile server is involved — your browser fetches only the bytes it needs to render the current view.

The AI assistant translates plain-English questions into SQL using DuckDB over the same Parquet files, with column schemas and dataset descriptions discovered automatically through a SpatioTemporal Asset Catalog (STAC).

Low-carbon, open models. The assistant is powered by open-weight large language models (GLM-4.7, Qwen3, GPT-OSS 120B, Kimi K2, MiniMax M2) running on the NRP open-llm-proxy, a shared university inference service running on hardware powered substantially by renewable energy. Compared to large commercial inference services, this approach reduces both the energy and the data-privacy footprint of every conversation. Carbon and energy use are tracked on our companion sustainability dashboard.

The full agent stack — map, chat, MCP tool server, and STAC catalog — is open-source. See the project repository and the geo-agent core library.

Working with the Data Assistant

The Data Assistant doesn't just answer questions — it can drive the map for you. Most of the things people reach for a settings menu to do can be asked in plain language:

  • Adjust layer styling and transparency: "Make Wildfire Perimeters 50% transparent", "Color CalEnviroScreen by pollution percentile", "Shade WCB projects by funding amount."
  • Show, hide, or filter layers: "Turn on Indigenous Territories and turn off Conservation Easements", "Show only fires after 2015", "Hide everything except Conservation Almanac sites in Senate District 2."
  • Zoom and pan: "Zoom to Assembly District 14", "Center the map on the Sierra Nevada."
  • Export query results: "Give me a CSV of WCB-funded projects in CD-16" — the assistant returns a downloadable table.
  • Combine layers in an analysis: "Which census tracts have both high pollution burden and low species richness?" — the assistant writes the spatial SQL and shows the result.

And a few interface tips that aren't visually obvious:

  • The Data Assistant panel is resizable — drag its left edge to give chat more or less width.
  • Use the header button to minimize the panel; a floating "show" button restores it.
  • On phones and tablets, the panel auto-collapses to overlay mode so the full map stays visible.
  • PMTiles layers don't use a fixed legend. Hover over any feature for a tooltip with that feature's live attributes — values, names, categories — direct from the source data.
  • Every tool call the assistant runs is logged in the per-turn timeline so you can see exactly what it queried, with what arguments, and what came back.

Data layers & sources

Every layer in the app draws from a public, primary source. Click "STAC" beside any entry to see the full machine-readable metadata — schemas, column descriptions, license, lineage, and download URLs.

Conservation Areas

TPL Conservation Almanac
Trust for Public Land — comprehensive record of conservation acquisitions and easements with funding source and program attribution.
STACSource
Conservation Ballot Measures (LandVote)
Trust for Public Land LandVote database — every state and local conservation finance measure put to voters since 1988.
STACSource
WCB Approved Projects
California Wildlife Conservation Board — all WCB-funded acquisitions, easements, and grants since 1949 (CDFW BIOS dataset ds672).
STACSource
CPAD Units & CCED Easements
California Protected Areas Database and California Conservation Easement Database, maintained by GreenInfo Network.
STAC: CPADSTAC: CCEDSource
Indigenous Territories (LandMark)
LandMark — global platform for indigenous and community lands, coordinated by the World Resources Institute and partners. Coverage reflects formal legal recognition only.
STACSource
PAD-US 4.1 (Fee & Combined)
US Geological Survey Gap Analysis Project — Protected Areas Database of the United States. Fee layer covers fee-owned protected areas; Combined layer adds easements, marine areas, and proclamation boundaries.
STAC: FeeSTAC: CombinedSource

American Rivers

Dam Removals
American Rivers Dam Removal Database — 2,126 documented dam removals across the United States, 1912–2025.
STACSource
Nationwide Rivers Inventory
US National Park Service — free-flowing river segments with outstanding natural, cultural, or recreational values potentially eligible for Wild & Scenic designation (2024 update).
STACSource
Active Protection Campaigns
American Rivers — 740 river reaches that are the focus of pending or active Wild & Scenic legislation.
STACSource
Eligible & Suitable Wild & Scenic Rivers
Federal land-management agency assessments (BLM, USFS, NPS, FWS) — 5,357 river segments evaluated for Wild & Scenic eligibility and suitability.
STAC
Designated Wild & Scenic Rivers
National Wild and Scenic Rivers System — 1,052 river segments permanently protected under the Wild and Scenic Rivers Act of 1968.
STACSource
IRA Drinking Water Watersheds
US EPA — HUC-12 watersheds influencing communities likely to benefit from Inflation Reduction Act drinking-water infrastructure funding.
STACSource
Rivers of Opportunity (CJEST)
Council on Environmental Quality — Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool, census-tract scores filtered for American Rivers' Rivers of Opportunity initiative.
STACSource

Carbon

Irrecoverable Carbon (2024)
The Nature Conservancy & Conservation International (Noon et al., Nature Sustainability 2022) — ecosystem carbon stocks that, if lost, cannot be recovered by 2050.
STACSource

Biodiversity

MOBI Species Richness
NatureServe Map of Biodiversity Importance — modeled species richness across all imperiled taxa in the United States.
STACSource
IUCN Species Richness (2025)
International Union for Conservation of Nature — Red List range maps rasterised to species-count grids for all terrestrial vertebrate taxa, with per-taxon breakdowns for mammals and birds.
STACSource
NCP Biodiversity Importance
Nature's Contributions to People — composite biodiversity-importance indicators; the "× Natural Habitat" variant masks the index to natural-habitat cover.
STAC

Demographics

Population Density (GHS-POP 2020)
Global Human Settlement Layer (European Commission JRC) — 2020 population count at ~90m resolution, R2023A release.
STACSource

Land Cover

Copernicus Global Land Cover (CGLS-LC100 2019)
Copernicus Global Land Service v3.0.1 — 100m global land-cover classification (23 classes, FAO LCCS scheme).
STACSource

Wetlands

Wetland Type (GLWD v2)
Global Lakes and Wetlands Database v2 (Lehner et al.) — 33 wetland classes derived from a global synthesis of inventories and remote sensing.
STACSource

Fire

Wildfire Perimeters & Prescribed Burns
CAL FIRE Fire and Resource Assessment Program (FRAP) — official California fire history (perimeters and treated-fuels), 2024 release.
STAC: PerimetersSTAC: RxBurnsSource
USGS Wildland Fires (1878–2020)
USGS Wildland Fire Combined Dataset 2021 — a national, deduplicated synthesis of wildfire perimeters from federal, state, and tribal sources extending back to the late 1800s.
STACSource

Social Vulnerability & Environmental Justice

Social Vulnerability Index (SVI 2022)
CDC / Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry — county- and tract-level vulnerability composite from 16 census variables.
STACSource
CalEnviroScreen 5.0
California Environmental Protection Agency, Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment — pollution and population-vulnerability screening for California census tracts.
STACSource
HOLC Redlining (Mapping Inequality)
Mapping Inequality (Robert K. Nelson et al., University of Richmond Digital Scholarship Lab) — Home Owners' Loan Corporation neighborhood security grades A–D from the late 1930s, the historical basis of redlining.
STACSource
CA Disadvantaged Communities (DAC 2023)
California Department of Water Resources — official DAC designation (median household income < 80% of statewide MHI, Water Code §79505.5(a)) at Census tract level. Used to determine eligibility for Proposition 1, Proposition 68, and SGMA water-funding programs.
STACSource

Political Boundaries

Congressional, Assembly & Senate Districts
US Census Bureau TIGER/Line — 119th Congress districts (2024) and California State Legislative Districts (2025).
STAC: CDSTAC: AssemblySTAC: SenateSource
California Census Tracts (2024)
US Census Bureau TIGER/Line 2024 — California tract boundaries, the standard sub-county geography used by ACS, CalEnviroScreen, SVI, and the DWR DAC layers.
