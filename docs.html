TPL California Conservation Data Explorer work in progress

An interactive map and AI assistant for exploring land conservation investment, public funding, and environmental data across California's congressional, assembly, and senate districts.

← Open the App

About this project

A research collaboration between the Department of Environmental Science, Policy, and Management at UC Berkeley and the Trust for Public Land, with support from NASA.

This app helps California legislators, conservation advocates, and researchers explore where public dollars and private partnerships have protected land — and which communities and ecosystems still need investment. The interface lets you toggle dozens of geospatial layers (conserved lands and 30x30 progress, ballot measures, biodiversity and habitat, connectivity, freshwater resources, climate risk, fire history, environmental justice scores) and ask natural-language questions that translate into live SQL queries against a federated catalog of cloud-native datasets.

This is a work in progress. Layers, models, and capabilities are being added and refined.

How it works

The map renders open geospatial datasets directly from cloud-native formats (PMTiles, Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs, GeoParquet) hosted on the National Research Platform. No tile server is involved — your browser fetches only the bytes it needs to render the current view.

The AI assistant translates plain-English questions into SQL using DuckDB over the same Parquet files, with column schemas and dataset descriptions discovered automatically through a SpatioTemporal Asset Catalog (STAC).

Low-carbon, open models. The assistant is powered by open-weight large language models (GLM-4.7, Qwen3, GPT-OSS 120B, Kimi K2, MiniMax M2) running on the NRP open-llm-proxy, a shared university inference service running on hardware powered substantially by renewable energy. Compared to large commercial inference services, this approach reduces both the energy and the data-privacy footprint of every conversation. Carbon and energy use are tracked on our companion sustainability dashboard.

The full agent stack — map, chat, MCP tool server, and STAC catalog — is open-source. See the project repository and the GLEN core library.

Working with the Data Assistant

If you wish the app could do something — just ask the assistant. Things people often reach for a settings menu to do (adjust transparency, export a CSV, filter a layer, run a cross-dataset query) the assistant can already do in plain language. If it can't, it will say so and usually suggest the closest thing it can.

A few of the actions people most often ask for:

  • Adjust layer styling and transparency: "Make Historic Fire Perimeters 50% transparent", "Color CalEnviroScreen by pollution percentile", "Shade WCB projects by funding amount."
  • Show, hide, or filter layers: "Turn on Indigenous Territories and turn off Conserved Areas", "Show only fires after 2015", "Hide everything except Conservation Almanac sites in Senate District 2."
  • Zoom and pan: "Zoom to Assembly District 14", "Center the map on the Sierra Nevada."
  • Query species occurrences and ranges: "Map the range of the California red-legged frog", "Which threatened species have GBIF records in Congressional District 16?" — IUCN and GBIF are queried per species and drawn as hex tiles on demand.
  • Export query results: "Give me a CSV of WCB-funded projects in CD-16" — the assistant returns a downloadable table.
  • Combine layers in an analysis: "Which census tracts have both high pollution burden and low species richness?" — the assistant writes the spatial SQL and shows the result.

And a few interface tips that aren't visually obvious:

  • The Data Assistant panel is resizable — drag its left edge to give chat more or less width.
  • Use the header button to minimize the panel; a floating "show" button restores it.
  • On phones and tablets, the panel auto-collapses to overlay mode so the full map stays visible.
  • PMTiles layers don't use a fixed legend. Hover over any feature for a tooltip with that feature's live attributes — values, names, categories — direct from the source data.
  • Every tool call the assistant runs is logged in the per-turn timeline — with timings — so you can see what it queried, with what arguments, what came back, and how long each step took.
✏️ Draw an area to ask about it. Use the draw tool on the map to sketch a polygon — a proposed acquisition, a watershed, a neighborhood, anywhere in California — then ask the assistant about it: "How much protected acreage falls inside the area I drew?", "Which conservation programs have funded projects in this polygon?", "What share of this area overlaps high-pollution communities in CalEnviroScreen?" Your drawn shape becomes a live spatial filter for any query.

Data layers & sources

Every layer in the app draws from a public, primary source. Click "STAC" beside any entry to see the full machine-readable metadata — schemas, column descriptions, license, lineage, and download URLs. Layer groups below mirror the toggle groups in the app.

Conservation Areas & 30x30 Progress

TPL Conservation Almanac (Sites & Funding)
Trust for Public Land — comprehensive record of conservation acquisitions and easements with funding source and program attribution. The companion funding table records the individual funding transactions behind each site.
STAC: SitesSTAC: FundingSource
Conserved Areas, Terrestrial (2025)
California Natural Resources Agency / CA Nature — the official 30x30 inventory of terrestrial conservation-area units, classified by GAP status (1–4). Only GAP 1 and 2 count toward the state's 30% conservation goal.
STACSource
Conservation Ballot Measures (LandVote)
Trust for Public Land LandVote database — every state and local conservation finance measure put to voters since 1988.
STACSource
WCB Approved Projects
California Wildlife Conservation Board — all WCB-funded acquisitions, easements, and grants since 1949 (CDFW BIOS dataset ds672).
STACSource
Indigenous Territories (LandMark)
LandMark — global platform for indigenous and community lands, coordinated by the World Resources Institute and partners. Coverage reflects formal legal recognition only.
STACSource

STAC
Plant Species Richness & Rarity-Weighted Endemic Richness
Kling et al. 2018 (via CA Nature) — California native vascular-plant richness and a rarity-weighted endemic-richness index highlighting concentrations of range-restricted endemics.

USFWS Critical Habitat (Final & Proposed)
US Fish & Wildlife Service — designated and proposed critical habitat for species listed under the Endangered Species Act.

Habitat Types (CWHR 60+ class)
CAL FIRE FVEG — California Wildlife Habitat Relationships classification of vegetation into 60+ habitat subtypes.

Ecoregions (20-class) & Climate Zones
CA Nature 30x30 ecoregions (CNRA / ACE Bailey-derived, 2024) and the Ackerly/Kling 10-class hierarchical-clustering climate zones used in the state's biodiversity and climate-resilience assessments.
STAC: EcoregionsSTAC: Climate Zones

ACE Biodiversity (CDFW)

Terrestrial Biodiversity Summary (ACE v3.0)
California Department of Fish and Wildlife — Areas of Conservation Emphasis terrestrial summary (BIOS ds2739): statewide and ecoregional BioRank and Rare Rank, plus native, rare, and endemic species-richness counts for amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals.
STACSource

Species Occurrences & Ranges (query-driven)

These are H3-hexed, per-species datasets with no pre-built map layer. Ask the assistant about a species or taxon and it queries the data and renders the result as hex tiles on demand.

IUCN Species Range Maps (2025)
IUCN Red List — expert range polygons for ~98,600 species converted to size-stratified H3 hexes. Use for "where does species X live" and "which species' ranges overlap area Y" questions.
STACSource
GBIF Occurrences (2026-06)
Global Biodiversity Information Facility — 3.5 billion georeferenced occurrence records hexed to H3, with taxonomic aggregations. Point observations of where species have actually been recorded. NonCommercial in aggregate.
STACSource

Water Utilities

Community Water System Service Areas (EPA SAB v3)
US EPA Office of Research and Development — service-area boundaries for 44,656 community drinking-water systems (CWS), covering ~99% of the US population served by community water systems. Approximately 60% are authoritative boundaries from state primacy agencies; the remainder are EPA-modeled (the Model_Method field records which). Each polygon carries a PWSID for joining to SDWIS.
STACSource
